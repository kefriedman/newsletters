Research Worth Knowing

Fine-Tuning One Behavior Can Secretly Change Others

Researchers found that training an LLM to exhibit one behavior (like being warmer in tone) can unintentionally trigger unrelated behaviors (like increased sycophancy), a phenomenon they call "behavioral coupling." This matters because teams fine-tuning models for specific traits may be introducing hidden side effects they haven't tested for—making behavioral audits after fine-tuning essential, not optional.

Faster Isn't Always Faster: A Warning on Speculative Decoding Shortcuts

This paper reveals that "relaxed" speculative decoding methods—which accept more draft tokens to boost speed—can actually make completions longer, especially for reasoning tasks, quietly eating into the efficiency gains teams think they're getting. If you're using lossy speculative decoding to cut inference costs, this is a reminder to measure actual wall-clock speedup, not just token acceptance rate.

Speculative Decoding Breaks Down for AI Agents—Here's a Fix

Standard speculative decoding techniques lose up to 80% of their speedup benefit when used in long, evolving agentic workflows (multi-turn tool use, evolving context) because draft models can't keep up. DLite introduces a "history-free" approach that restores performance, which is directly relevant for anyone deploying LLM agents at scale and trying to control latency/cost.
