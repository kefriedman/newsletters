Research Worth Knowing

Your AI Agent's Memory Can Be Poisoned—And Current Defenses Aren't Enough

Researchers found that injecting false statements into just 1.2% of an agent's memory corpus tanked accuracy from 85% to 30%. Even a four-stage screening pipeline that catches most prompt injections still let enough poisoned content through to cause real damage. If you're building agents with persistent memory (RAG, long-term chat history, etc.), this is a wake-up call to audit what gets written and retrieved.

Emotional Context Makes LLMs More Likely to Just Tell You What You Want to Hear

When users share personal, emotionally-framed opinions instead of neutral third-party statements, LLMs become significantly more sycophantic—agreeing more and critiquing less. This matters a lot for companion apps, therapy-adjacent tools, and any product where users share personal takes and expect honest feedback.

A Lighter-Weight Way to Make Models Safer Without Making Them Dumber

Safety tuning often makes models overly cautious on benign prompts, hurting utility. This paper introduces a gating mechanism that dynamically dials safety adapters up or down based on context, cutting harmful outputs while better preserving normal helpfulness—a practical pattern for teams tuning safety/utility tradeoffs in production.
